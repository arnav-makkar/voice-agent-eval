The argument, in one page

read this before you touch a terminal

Sarvam has published a task success rate exactly once. It appears in the EkStep Listen at Scale report, against a deployment called SwaTaleem: 62% task success rate. One number, one programme, out of twenty organisations and 74 lakh voice minutes.

Everything else they publish is reach. Calls placed, minutes served, conversations that lasted more than fifteen seconds, users identified. Those are real numbers and they are impressive — 1.58 million calls for Maharashtra's Aaple Sarkar, 1.1 lakh structured interactions for Piramal, 2.5 million outreach calls for the National Health Authority. But almost none of it says whether the call worked.

Why these three domains

Two of them are the same mechanic, and that mechanic is Sarvam's single most-deployed pattern.

DOMAIN B · BFSI

Inbound. Authenticate, return fund value and premium due date, complete the payment intent or hand off — without stating a surrender value the core system did not return.
DOMAIN C · SCALE

Outbound. Collect feedback on an enrolment or update, including whether the resident was overcharged. Ten languages, air-gapped, integrity flagging.

A and C are the same machine. Both are outbound structured-feedback collection with an integrity flag and a completion criterion. Build A properly and C costs you a schema swap, not a rebuild. That is the engineering argument for why three domains fit in two weeks, and it is also the product argument: this is one reusable environment, not three demos.

B is the different one — inbound, transactional, regulated, and the only domain where a wrong number is a compliance event rather than a bad experience. It is there because BFSI is where Sarvam earns, and because it forces the verifier to prove something harder than "did we collect the answers."

★ Sarvam has already shipped your architecture — in another vertical

The Government of Maharashtra Aaple Sarkar deployment, from Sarvam's own EkStep report: 1.58M+ calls, 490K+ meaningful conversations, 53K+ with qualitative feedback, 35 districts, average service rating 4.37/5. The agent "verified beneficiary identity, captured service ratings, audited whether multiple visits were required, and flagged reports of unofficial transaction costs."

Identity check → structured capture → integrity flag. That is exactly Domain A and Domain C. You are not proposing a new pattern; you are proposing the measurement layer for the pattern they run most.

What you measure

the metric spine, and why every choice is defensible

The rung above their rung

Sarvam published its own evaluation stack on 2 April 2026 — the last thing they posted before Epoch. Their Intent Score is binary, LLM-judged, and asks: did the system capture what the speaker was trying to say? That scores the utterance.

TSR scores the call. Say it in exactly those terms and it stops being a metric you invented and becomes the next rung on a ladder they already built.

THEIRS · UTTERANCE

THEIRS · UTTERANCE

0–1, threshold ≥0.90. They name banking as a primary use case
THEIRS · UTTERANCE

WER rescored so code-mix script choices stop counting as errors. Threshold <15%

Task Success Rate
DB end-state hash × required-output containment. No LLM anywhere in the reward
MetricDefinitionWhy it survives scrutiny
TSR north star Product of DB and COMMUNICATE. DB = hash of the canonicalised end-state database against a target produced by replaying a reference trajectory on a fresh environment. COMMUNICATE = every required string appeared in the agent's turns. Outcome-based, not path-based — any tool sequence reaching an equivalent state passes, which avoids SABER's most common defect (over-constrained gold paths, ~26% of their 42 documented corrections). And a reviewer can disagree with a judge; they cannot disagree with a hash.
pass^k how you report it Probability that all k independent trials of a task succeed. Minimum 3 trials per task, reported with per-task distribution and standard error. The user simulator is stochastic. A single-trial delta of a few points is noise, and saying so is worth more than a bigger number. Note: NeMo Gym does not compute pass^k — that's a contribution you can make.
Refusal fidelity Domain A + C Share of refused questions recorded as refusals rather than as zeros or invented values. The single most important field in a feedback agent, and completely invisible to every engagement metric. A 0 and a refusal look identical to a containment rate.
Integrity-flag recall Domain C Of tasks where the resident reports an overcharge, the share where flag_overcharge fired with the correct delta. Directly mirrors Aaple Sarkar's finding of "middleman interference in 2.75% of interactions." Detecting and not logging is the worst outcome, and only an end-state check catches it.
Ungrounded-value rate Domain B Share of calls where the agent stated a figure the tool layer did not return. The BFSI hallucination case, made mechanically detectable. Zero tolerance — and a containment metric would have scored those calls as successes.
Escalation correctness Domain B Correct handoffs / (correct handoffs + missed handoffs + spurious handoffs). SBI Life state a human-in-the-loop framework. Correct escalation is a pass, not a failure. Most containment-driven metrics punish exactly the behaviour a regulated insurer wants.
partial_action_reward diagnostic m of n reference actions matched, split by READ vs WRITE. SABER: "each additional deviation in a mutating action reduces the odds of success by up to 92%; deviations in non-mutating actions have little to no effect." An eval that weights every step equally is measuring the wrong thing.
ΔTSR under ASR degradation the honest audio proxy Inject word errors at known rates into the transcript path, re-run, report the curve. You are TTS-ing inputs, so you have no real disfluencies, barge-in or endpointing. Making ASR degradation a controlled perturbation is defensible; pretending text eval covers speech is not.

SABER audited τ-bench and found annotation errors capped achievable performance at ~70% on airline and ~92% on retail, with 62% of airline and 46% of retail instructions under-specified — and noted "these problems persist even in the recently released τ²-Bench." Sierra AI, who invented the method, got it wrong at scale.

So: run the adversarial pilot before you believe any number, and publish your own defect rate. A demo that reports "we found and fixed 11 broken tasks out of 60" is far more credible than one that implies the task set was right first time.

The 14 days

every day has a gate — if the gate fails, cut scope, don't extend
⚠ Day 2 decides whether this plan is possible at all

At 30–120 seconds per multi-turn rollout, a 1,500-rollout optimiser run is 12 to 50 hours serial. You need 8–16-way parallelism to fit one into an afternoon — and DSPy issue #9150 documents that stateful benchmark environments are frequently process-bound and not thread-safe.

Prove parallel throughput on day 2. If you can't get 8-way, drop the GEPA arm on day 2 and reallocate those days to task quality. That is a better demo than a half-finished optimiser run.

⚠ The three ways this plan actually fails — and the arithmetic behind each

1. The adversarial pilot eats the schedule. 60 tasks × 40 trials is 2,400 rollouts. At 60 seconds each that is 40 hours serial, or 5 hours at 8-way parallelism. Feasible — but only if day 2 works. Mitigation: 40 trials is τ-bench's number for a 115-task set. For your smaller domains, 20 trials finds broken tasks nearly as well. Drop to 20 on B and C without apology.

2. Days 1–4 assume fluency you don't have yet. Authoring a τ³ domain means five files plus db.json, policy.md, tasks.json and registry wiring, in a codebase with 144 open issues. Mitigation: day 1 exists precisely to run someone else's domain first. If day 3 ends without a passing gold trajectory, cut Domain B on day 4 rather than compressing the pilot — a two-domain demo with a validated task set beats three domains you can't defend.

3. Cost is the one thing that isn't a risk. Roughly 8,000–10,000 rollouts across the whole fortnight. On Sarvam-30B at ₹2.5 in / ₹10 out per 1M tokens, that is tens of dollars, not hundreds — and running the demo on the model that powers Samvaad is the right call anyway. Keep a frontier model for the GEPA reflector only; a cheap reflector fails outright.

◆ Cut lines, in order — decide these in advance, not at 2am on day 12
  1. First to go: the GEPA arm. If day 2's parallelism spike fails, drop it immediately and say why on camera. "I measured the rollout throughput, and a credible optimiser run was 40 hours serial, so I spent those days on task quality instead" is a good answer.
  2. Second: audio-native. Keep the ASR-degradation curve, which is cheap and honest, and drop the full duplex runs.
  3. Third: Domain B. Painful, because BFSI is the anchor — but A and C together still make the argument, and they share a verifier.
  4. Never cut: the adversarial pilot, the frozen split, the ≥3 trials, or the licence table. Those four are what make it real.

The honesty slide — say all of this out loud

What this is

Simulated conversations, not PSTN traffic.

Mocked backends, not core banking or UIDAI systems.

Roughly 120 tasks total, not millions of calls.

Text-first rollouts, with audio validation on the winning configuration only.

Dialogue arrangement synthetic; every entity cited and licence-checked.

What it nonetheless proves

That objective completion can be scored without an LLM judge.

That refusal fidelity and integrity flags are measurable, and invisible to engagement metrics.

That the breaking turn is findable and reproducible on demand.

That the same environment generalises across three unrelated domains.

That the whole thing re-runs from one command, by someone who doesn't trust you.

★ Saying this is not a weakness — it is the demonstration

You are pitching a company whose most persistent public criticism is that its performance claims are self-reported. A demo that lists its own limitations before anyone asks is the argument, performed.

τ-bench's authors did the same thing — SABER later found 42 ground-truth errors in their set, and the field respects them more, not less, for having shipped a checkable artefact in the first place. Be the person who publishes the defect rate.

The three findings to lead with, in order

FINDING 01
The metric nobody has
Refusal fidelity. A recorded zero and a refusal look identical to every engagement metric Sarvam publishes — and across 74 lakh voice minutes of feedback collection, that distinction is the whole dataset's validity.
FINDING 02
The gap between control and regular
Same agent, same tasks, clean audio versus noise and accents. The delta separates acoustic failure from reasoning failure — and on Hinglish it will be wide. VoiceAgentBench found exactly this pattern for Indic.
FINDING 03
Your own defect rate
How many of your tasks were broken, and how you found them. Sierra AI shipped 42 documented errors; SWE-bench Verified filtered out 68.3% of its samples. Publishing yours is the credential.
