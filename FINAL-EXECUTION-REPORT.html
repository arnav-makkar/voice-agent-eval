Closed learning loop

Evaluation and improvement are separate jobs.

“Self-improving” means the system repeatedly proposes and validates bounded changes. It does not mean the production agent edits itself without governance.

01IngestCalls, transcripts, tools, state, versions, latency, run_id.
02ContractDomain outcomes, forbidden actions, required tools, experience floors.
03EvaluateRun stateful episodes; score execution truth and conversation.
04LocalizeFind first break and assign the owning component.
05DeriveCreate failure, counterfactual, boundary, and regression cases.
06ImproveManual repair, GEPA, extractor, tool, workflow, model, voice arms.
07Re-evaluateSame frozen contracts; exact paired comparison.
08Release gateReject any P0/P1 regression; preserve baseline wins.
09MonitorCanary, drift, business outcome, rollback, next cycle.

Release controller

  4. Human + voice gatesProvider commit, matched audio, owner approval, canary, rollback.

Claim policy

What you can defend in the room.

The fastest way to lose credibility is to turn a good MVP into a claim the evidence cannot support.

Safe claims

  • I built an executable, case-agnostic evaluation and improvement framework.
  • On a frozen 30-case development suite, v15 moved exact task success from 16 to 30 with zero task regression.
  • On a sealed 12-case final, the frozen candidate moved 5 to 9 with four repairs and zero observed task regression.
  • GEPA produced a strong 28/30 arm, and the release gate correctly rejected its severe regressions.
  • The framework records run IDs, tool/state truth, first failure, prompt/evaluator hashes, MLflow lineage, and release decisions.

Do not claim yet

  • “The voice agent improved by 33 points.” The final result is text-mode.
  • “The result is statistically significant.” Fresh-final p = 0.125.
  • “This increased collections.” Cash settlement is not observable.
  • “It will directly work on one million SBI/LIC calls.” It needs domain contracts, sampling, calibration, privacy and scale infrastructure.
  • “The system autonomously deploys.” Human approval, matched voice, canary and rollback are deliberate gates.

Two-minute interview pitch

Lead with the system, prove with EMI.

“I built Loopline, a self-improving agent framework for Sarvam Indus. It separates evaluation, improvement, and release so an optimizer can never grade or promote itself. I started with 20 real calls for failure discovery, then converted those patterns into versioned stateful scenarios with hidden truth, executable promise and callback tools, guardrails, experience metrics, and first-failure localization. Failures are routed to the component that owns them; prompt repair is only one option. I ran manual and GEPA arms, logged every run in MLflow, and rejected GEPA’s 28/30 finalist because it introduced a P0 guardrail regression. The safe v15 arm scored 30/30 on development. I then froze the prompt, evaluator, selector and suite, generated a new 12-case final, and allowed baseline and candidate one pass each. The result was 5 to 9 wins, no task regression, with a clear p=0.125 limitation. I also built a Sarvam CALL adapter and six-condition acoustic diagnostic, but I do not claim a voice lift until the selected prompt is approved, deployed, and evaluated in a matched round. The same framework can move to hospital or banking use cases by replacing the domain contract, tools, simulator and policy—not the learning loop.”
