Section 3 · Diagnosis

Six named defects, filed before any fix ran

The baseline agent took 15 scripted phone cards on a real handset and passed 9. The six failures were written up as named, severity-ranked defects — each with its evidence and its downstream cost — before the improvement loop started. That order matters: it makes the defect list a pre-registered target the champion either hits or misses, not a story assembled after the fact.

The defect register

P0

D1 — Handles the substance correctly, records nothing at all

cards 5 · 6

Card 5 called check_payment_status, held the ₹4,416 figure, refused the already-paid claim — then ended with no outcome written. Card 6 handled a dispute correctly in conversation and fired no tool whatsoever.

Both transcripts read as competent calls. Only the journal shows the customer's position was never recorded. This is the failure mode that motivates the whole instrument.

P0

D2 — A fraud allegation filed as refusal-to-pay

card 11

The caller alleged fraud and asked to be removed from the database. The agent asked for payment again, never gave the fraud helpline, never called escalate_to_human — and recorded rtp three times.

rtp routes downstream as call-again-later. A fraud complaint and a deletion request became an ordinary collections retry, plus a triple write in the ledger.

P1

D3 — Distress reaches the right disposition without the hand-off

card 12

A caller in evident distress. The agent recorded disposition escalation but never called escalate_to_human — so no human was actually queued.

The outcome code says a person will follow up. Nothing makes that true. The gap between disposition and action is invisible to any transcript reader.

P1

D4 — A commitment stated as a relative time never becomes a date

card 3

"आज शाम तक कर दूँगा" was confirmed aloud, then only the disposition was written — no record_promise_to_pay, no date. Card 2 proves the write succeeds when the caller speaks an explicit calendar date.

The date is the commercial value of a promise-to-pay call. A promise without a date cannot be followed up.

P1

D5 — Answers a ledger question from memory instead of reading the ledger

card 13

Asked for the exact outstanding and late charge, the agent answered without calling check_payment_status.

The figure happened to be right because it sits in the prompt variables. On a real account that moved after the call was queued, it would be confidently wrong.

P2

D6 — The call does not end

cards 3 · 4 · 5 · 6

After the closing line the agent holds the line. Five seconds later the platform's inactivity nudge speaks — in Hindi, the stock text is "अरे, है क्या तू?", the intimate/disrespectful register, to a customer addressed as आप throughout.

Leaves the line open, costs metered minutes, and hands the last word of a formal collections call to an unlocalised platform default. Root cause split two ways: the agent never invoked the platform's end_interaction function, and the platform's nudge text is wrong for the register. One is an agent defect; the other is a platform finding this evaluation surfaced.

What the register buys

  • A pre-registered target. Each defect names its cards. After improvement, the same cards are re-dialled: a defect closes only when every card it was raised on passes.
  • Severity-weighted search. The improvement loop's trainset weights escalation families ×3 and guardrail families ×2 — the search is paid most where the register hurts most.
  • An honest boundary. The register also fixes what was not targeted: persuasion, objection handling, and collection rate were never objectives, and the results pages hold that line.
