1 · The one defect that dominated

The baseline was not incompetent and not rude. It said the right thing and failed to write it down. A promise was made and never recorded; an escalation code was filed without ever escalating; a balance question was answered from the prompt instead of the ledger. Because tool truth is read from an append-only journal, never from the transcript, these were all visible as the same shape of failure.

The rule that carried the result

An earlier, discarded experiment had needed a human to write this rule by hand. Given per-scenario journal evidence and the baseline's own counterfactual verdicts, the optimiser derived it unaided:

**Turn one — record.** Call `record_call_outcome` with the code that matches how
the call actually ended. Say nothing else in this turn.
**NEVER call `record_call_outcome` in the same turn as any other tool.**
IMPORTANT: You cannot log `ptp_today`, `fptp`, `callback`, `dispute`, or
`escalation` unless you have already called the corresponding business tool.
If you missed it, call the business tool NOW instead, say a brief
acknowledgment, and call `record_call_outcome` on your next turn.

It also closed the long-standing "agent won't hang up" defect on its own:

When the outcome is clear (e.g., they committed, refused, disputed, or required
escalation), move to close immediately without waiting for the customer to say goodbye.

And it made the outcome codes self-enforcing

`ptp_today` — they commit to paying later today (requires `record_promise_to_pay` with `currentDate`).
`fptp` — they commit to a specific future date (requires `record_promise_to_pay` with that exact date).
`callback` — they ask to be called back at a stated time (requires `schedule_callback`).
`already_paid` — if they insist they already paid, requires calling `check_payment_status` first.

3 · Generation 2 — one example, one family

the finding  Generation 2 did not change the instructions at all. Byte-for-byte identical to generation 1 — all eight sections, same lengths. Its entire contribution was +11 lines in the second component: one rule and one worked example, both about customers claiming they had already paid.

- **Ledger & Payment Claims**: You MUST call `check_payment_status` BEFORE answering any
question about balance, OR immediately if the customer claims they have already paid.
**already paid claim** (from a real call, EMI-BENCH-0054):
Customer: "पैसा तो कट गया मेरे खाते से"
Shubh: "अच्छा, मैं एक बार check कर लेता हूँ। क्या आप मुझे बता सकते हैं कि payment कब हुई थी?"

5 · How the change was produced

Seedcommitted v3 instructions, verbatim, plus worked examples mined from its own passing calls
SearchGEPA proposes a candidate; Gemini 3.1 Pro reflects on per-scenario journal evidence
Applycandidate PUT to the live draft over the authoring API in ~1 second
Scorereal conversations with the deployed agent; reward read from the append-only journal
Gateblind splits, pre-measured noise floor, McNemar, guardrail veto

1,570 real conversations. The reward is never the transcript — a spoken claim with no matching journal write scores zero, which is why "said it but didn't record it" was findable at all.

6 · What this does not claim

  • Compounding is not established. Gen 1 → Gen 2 is +5 overall at p = 0.18. Positive and regression-free, but the honest sentence is "a second cycle helped slightly and broke nothing" — not that improvement compounds indefinitely.
  • Voice is unmeasured. Every figure here is the text channel of a voice agent. The transfer test — 15 human phone calls and 5 bot-to-bot on this champion — has not been run.
  • Surface form matters a little. With every caller line reworded, the champion drops 29→26 while the baseline drops 18→17. It keeps ~82% of its advantage, so the gain is mostly wording-independent tool discipline — but not entirely.
  • One account, scripted callers, uniform family mix. Correct for coverage of rare and dangerous cases; not a forecast of production traffic.
